Coaching Tales

Bringing the histories of women in football coaching to life through interactive digital storytelling.

How can historical research reach people beyond the academy? Coaching Tales explores this question through an interactive web application that invites audiences to discover the lives and experiences of women football coaches across the twentieth century.

Project Digital research prototype
Focus Women in football coaching history
Funding £5,000 targeted funding from the NWCDTP
Partners Manchester Metropolitan University and the National Football Museum
01

The idea

Making overlooked histories accessible

My PhD research explores the history of women in football coaching, with a particular aim to reveal, record and represent women whose experiences have often been missing from dominant narratives about gender and sport.

My collaborative doctoral project with the National Football Museum uncovered stories with the potential to reach audiences well beyond academic research. I wanted to explore how these histories could be shared in a format that felt engaging and relevant to people encountering them for the first time.

With £5,000 in targeted funding from the North West Consortium Doctoral Training Partnership (NWCDTP), I developed the idea for Coaching Tales: a web application that translates historical research into an interactive experience. The funding supported the development of the first prototype, alongside image copyright and associated technical costs.

02

Research into experience

Three lives, different eras

The prototype centres on three women whose coaching lives span distinct periods of the twentieth century: Irene Taylor in the interwar period, Bobbie Leigh in the 1960s, and Michele Robinson in the 1980s. Together, their stories offer ways to explore changing opportunities for women in football and wider British society.

The stories draw on different forms of historical evidence. Irene Taylor's and Bobbie Leigh's biographies were developed from archival and genealogical sources and written in the first person. Their voices were then represented using, respectively, a voice actor and a computer-generated voice.

Michele Robinson's story takes a different approach. As a pioneering coach whose experiences I explored through oral history, her biography incorporates excerpts from her own interview testimony. This gives users the opportunity to hear her speak for herself, while creating space to consider the differences between original testimony and recreated historical voices.

Bringing these approaches together allowed me to explore how historical stories can be told digitally and how the medium of a story affects the relationship between a person, their testimony and an audience.

03

Design and interaction

A story shaped by the user

Rather than presenting each biography as a single linear account, Coaching Tales gives users choices about what they want to discover. After selecting a coach, users can explore two pathways: one focused on football coaching and another on culture and wider influences.

Each pathway contains themed topics, with audio at the centre of the experience and relevant images helping to situate each story. Transcripts accompany the audio to support accessibility and different ways of engaging with the material.

The choices users make are recorded and presented at the end of the experience. They can return to a biography and explore a different route, encountering material they may not have heard the first time.

This branching structure was a deliberate design choice. It encourages exploration while reflecting a wider feature of historical research: the stories we encounter are shaped by what has been recorded and selected for attention.

04

Public engagement

Testing the prototype with young audiences

On International Women's Day, 8 March 2025, I organised a field trip for the National Saturday Club, a group of young people aged 13–16 facilitated by Manchester Metropolitan University. The visit included a tour of the National Football Museum, educational activities and time to explore Coaching Tales.

Informal feedback from participants offered an early opportunity to understand how the prototype worked as a public-facing research output. The interactive format was well received, and participants highlighted the value of hearing a real person speak in Michele Robinson's story. One participant reflected:

“I genuinely learnt quite a lot from it.”

Participants also suggested possibilities for further development, including quizzes and video, and expressed enthusiasm for hearing stories from more coaches. The transcripts were welcomed as a useful feature for different learning styles.

This feedback helped identify opportunities to develop the experience further while reinforcing the value of presenting historical research in a format that invites people to take an active role in discovering it.

05

Reflection

Exploring what digital history can do

Coaching Tales brought together historical research, oral testimony, creative writing, audio and interactive design in a single public-facing prototype. It gave me an opportunity to experiment with how research can be translated into a different form without losing sight of the people and experiences at its centre.

The project also raised important questions about voice, authenticity and historical representation. How might a recreated voice shape a listener's sense of the past? What changes when a person can speak through their own recorded testimony? And how can interactive choices encourage exploration without obscuring the complexity of historical lives?

The first version is a starting point rather than a finished platform. Future development could expand the range of biographies, introduce additional forms of interaction and bring the experience to more audiences.
