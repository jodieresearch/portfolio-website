Oral history · Qualitative research · Women's football

Listening to
Experience

Exploring women's experiences of football coaching through oral history.

As part of my PhD research, I conducted oral history interviews with women involved in football coaching before 2000. I used their personal accounts to explore how women navigated the culture, opportunities and constraints of coaching across different periods and places in Britain.

Context PhD research
Method Semi-structured oral history
Participants Seven women
Period explored Before 2000
01

The question

What can personal stories tell us about the history of coaching?

Historical accounts can tell us about policies and institutions. But they do not always capture how people experienced these changes in their everyday lives.

My research explored how women understood their own experiences of football coaching: the opportunities they encountered, the barriers they negotiated, and the relationships that shaped their involvement.

I was interested in what had happened and in how participants made sense of their experiences.

02

Research design

Creating space for participants' own narratives.

I designed and conducted semi-structured interviews, using broadly chronological prompts to guide conversations while allowing participants to shape their own accounts.

The interviews explored coaching experiences, qualifications, cultural norms and values, and the significance participants attached to their involvement in the game.

01

Designed the interview approach

Developed prompts around key research themes, while leaving room for unexpected memories and reflections.

02

Conducted in-depth interviews

Interviewed seven women with varied experiences of coaching and involvement in football.

03

Prioritised participant voice

Kept interviewer interjections to a minimum, allowing participants to identify what mattered to them and construct their own life narratives.

03

Ethics & practice

Research built on care and informed participation.

The interview process was undertaken following institutional ethics approval. Participants were provided with information about the study and gave written consent before taking part.

I was attentive to the personal nature of oral history: participants were not simply sources of information, but individuals reflecting on experiences that had shaped their lives.

This meant creating an interview environment in which participants could speak openly and decide which experiences they considered significant.

04

Analysis

Connecting individual memories with wider histories.

I brought the interviews into conversation with historical research into women's football coaching and its wider cultural context.

Through collective biography, I considered the testimonies together: identifying shared experiences while recognising differences in participants' lives and interpretations.

This approach allowed personal accounts to reveal broader patterns in women's coaching, including informal mentorship, cultural expectations and the ways women understood their own legacy.

Individual memories can reveal histories that institutional records alone cannot fully capture.

Research in practice

Listening closely. Interpreting carefully.

This work developed my experience in qualitative research, interview design, ethical practice and the interpretation of personal testimony.

I bring a thoughtful and rigorous approach to working with people's experiences.
